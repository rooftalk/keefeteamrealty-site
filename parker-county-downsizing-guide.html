Downsizing in Parker County, Texas

By Lisa Keefe, REALTOR® · Broker Associate, JLA Realty · Seniors Real Estate Specialist® (SRES®)

If we were sitting at your kitchen table, I'd ask what you want to make easier. Is it the stairs? The yard? The drive to appointments in Weatherford? Or is everyone worried about what happens to the house if Mom can't manage it on her own?

You can talk through those questions before you decide whether to sell. I'm a Broker Associate with JLA Realty and a Seniors Real Estate Specialist®. I help Parker County families look at what it would take to stay put, move somewhere easier to manage, or share a home with family. I'd start with the way you live now and put real numbers next to each option.

Would it be easier to stay in the house?

Walk through an ordinary Tuesday. Carry the groceries in from the car. Look at the step into the shower, the path to the laundry, and the lighting between the bedroom and the bathroom. A better handrail, a step-free entry, yard help, or a regular ride into Weatherford may solve the problem that started the whole conversation. On acreage, add the well, septic, fences, and driveway to the list of things someone has to keep up with.

If you're looking at help at home or a senior community, ask what's included, what costs extra, and what happens if you need more help later. If a hospital discharge is driving the decision, ask the discharge planner which kind of care is needed and who pays for it. Medicare can cover certain skilled care when you meet its rules. It generally doesn't pay for ongoing help with everyday tasks like bathing and dressing.

Medicare's long-term care guide · Medicare Care Compare

For help finding local services, the North Central Texas Aging and Disability Resource Center serves Parker County and can point you toward resources for care at home and caregiver support.

North Central Texas Aging and Disability Resource Center

Will a smaller home actually cost less?

I'd put two numbers on paper. First, what would you keep from the sale after the mortgage payoff, selling costs, and any repairs you plan to make? Then, what would it cost to live in the next place? Include the new tax bill, insurance, utilities, upkeep, and any HOA dues or community fees. Give moving, storage, and any changes you'd need to make before moving in their own line.

A one-story house in Willow Park may be easier to live in. Whether it saves money depends on the address and the numbers. Ask for an estimate based on your ownership and exemptions before you build a budget around the seller's tax bill.

What happens to my Texas property taxes when I turn 65?

For the 2026 tax year, a qualifying Texas homeowner age 65 or older can get an extra $60,000 school district homestead exemption on top of the $140,000 general school district homestead exemption. Together, they take $200,000 off the home's appraised value for school tax purposes. On a qualifying home appraised at $350,000, that would leave $150,000 taxable for school district purposes before any other applicable adjustments. City, county, and other local taxes have their own rules.

Texas Comptroller's homestead exemption amounts

The school tax ceiling is a separate benefit. Think of the exemption as lowering the value the school district taxes and the ceiling as putting a limit on the school tax dollars you owe. The ceiling generally starts with the school district tax in the year you first qualify. Your school tax can be lower, and major additions may raise the ceiling. Ask which other local taxing units, if any, offer their own ceiling at your address.

Texas Comptroller's guide to tax ceilings

Can I take my school tax ceiling with me if I move?

If you move to another qualifying Texas homestead, you can generally transfer the percentage of school tax you were paying under the ceiling. Say your old school tax was $1,000 under the ceiling and would have been $2,000 without it. That's 50%. In that simplified example, if the new home's school tax would otherwise be $1,600, applying the same percentage would put the new ceiling at $800. Ask your current appraisal district for a tax ceiling certificate and check with the new district about applying it and filing your homestead exemption.

Texas school tax ceiling certificate

For a Parker County address, I'd call the Parker County Appraisal District at 817-596-0077 with both addresses or property IDs. Ask which taxing units serve each property, whether your age-65 exemption and ceiling are on file, and what you need for the transfer. If the next property is in another county, call that appraisal district too.

Can we sell an inherited house in Parker County?

Before I'd set a listing date, I'd want the family to know who has legal authority to sign. Say Dad left the house to three kids and one of them lives there. I'd ask a Texas probate attorney and the title company to review the deed and estate papers before the family promises a move-out date or spends money on repairs. The answer can depend on how the deed was written, whether there's a will or trust, and what the court has already done.

  • Find the papers. Pull together the recorded deed, death certificate, original will if there is one, any trust or transfer-on-death deed, and any court papers already issued. Add the mortgage statement, tax bill, insurance information, and survey if you have it.
  • Ask which path fits. Probate is the court process that may be needed to establish the will or handle the estate. If there's no will, Texas inheritance law applies. An attorney can tell you whether a court heirship proceeding, an affidavit of heirship, or another route fits. Have the title company confirm what it needs to insure the sale. A power of attorney used during someone's lifetime ends when that person dies. If a will needs probate, Texas generally has a four-year deadline from the date of death, with limited exceptions. Ask early, even if the family plans to keep the property for now. Texas Estates Code, Chapter 256 · TexasLawHelp's heirship guide
  • Keep the house running while you sort it out. Decide who will keep up with the utilities, mortgage, insurance, and yard. If the house is vacant, ask the insurer how that affects the policy. Keep a record of expenses and ask the estate attorney before selling or giving away belongings if authority is still being sorted out.

The Parker County Clerk is where I'd look for recorded deeds on Parker County property. If the probate case belongs in Parker County, the County Probate Court page points you to local filing information. Your attorney can confirm where the case belongs.

Parker County Clerk · Parker County Probate Court

What if we want to live with family?

Talk through privacy, guests, parking, bills, and what help everyone can realistically give. If you're counting on a guest house or garage conversion, check deed restrictions, permits, and water or septic capacity before you count it as living space. If one person is putting money into a home someone else owns, have a Texas attorney help put the arrangement in writing, including what happens if someone needs to move or sell later.

How can a trailer help with a downsizing move?

Measure the rooms, doors, and hallways at the next place before you decide what goes. A tape measure can settle the dining table question faster than a family vote. Then sort one room at a time. Mark what goes to the new home, what goes to family, and what can be donated or sold.

This is where I'd talk with you about the trailer. A small load of boxes or a few pieces set aside for family can go on a separate trip, so you have fewer decisions and less to handle on the main moving day. If a table is going to one person and boxes are going to another, label them by stop. Tell me what you're trying to move and where it's going, and we can talk through whether the trailer fits the job and what needs a mover. Keep medications, keys, and important papers with you.

What should we talk about while we have time?

North Side Baptist Church's Family Matters Handbook calls this "the other talk." I'd start with three questions. What do you want to keep doing on your own? Who would you trust to help with money or medical decisions if you needed it? Where would that person find the papers?

Ask a Texas estate planning or elder law attorney about your will or trust, financial and medical powers of attorney, and advance directives. Keep the signed papers secure and tell the people you've chosen where to find them. While you're together, write down who should be called first in an emergency and any wishes you want the family to know about care, final arrangements, or keepsakes. You can come back to the rest over another cup of coffee.
