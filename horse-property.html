Buying horse property: what we look at that most agents don't.

We've both lived the horse-property life for decades. These are the questions we walk through with every buyer before they fall in love with a place.

Before you start looking for horse property…

If buying horse property is one of your goals, don't start by scrolling listings. Start by making a list of how you actually want to live.

How many horses do you see yourself having? What kind of riding do you want to do? Will you keep horses at home full-time? Do you want to be close to shows, trainers, playday events or 4-H activities? How far are you willing to drive to work, school and everything else in your life?

Once we understand those answers, we can help you separate the things you truly need from the things that would simply be nice to have. That's usually where a productive horse-property search begins.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Define the lifestyle first. Then find the property that supports it.

What does the deed actually allow?

A property can look absolutely perfect for horses and still have restrictions that don't work for what you have planned. Before falling in love with a property, we want to know if there are deed restrictions, HOA restrictions, city ordinances or other limitations that could affect horses, barns, arenas or other improvements.

This is one of those things that's much better to discover before you buy rather than after you've moved in.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Never assume that because you see acreage, or even horses next door, that you can use your property the same way.

Where will you ride?

This is something first-time horse-property buyers don't always think about. Owning a horse and having somewhere to actually ride your horse are two different things.

Is there room for an arena? Are there trails nearby? Can you safely ride on your own property? Will you need to trailer somewhere every time you want to ride? We like to ask these questions early because the answer can completely change which property is the best fit.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Think beyond where your horse will live and consider where you're actually going to enjoy riding.

Can you get a horse trailer in and out?

A long, winding driveway through the trees may look beautiful in listing photos. It may not be quite as charming the first time you're backing a horse trailer down it!

When we look at horse properties, we're thinking about things a typical homebuyer may never consider: driveway width, overhead branches, gates, turning radius and whether there's enough room to maneuver a truck and trailer.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: When you tour a horse property, picture arriving home after dark with horses in the trailer. Could you comfortably get everyone unloaded?

Where will the hay go?

This sounds like such a little thing until you've had a load of hay delivered and realize you don't have a good place to put it.

Hay needs to stay dry and accessible, but you also want to think about fire safety and how easily you can get to your storage area. Feed, supplements, tack and equipment all need somewhere to go, too. These are exactly the practical details we look at when we're evaluating a property with horse buyers.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Don't just count stalls. Look at the storage space you'll need to actually operate the place.

Meet your neighbors before you buy

Country neighbors can be wonderful, but it's worth learning what's around a property before you buy it. Are there loose dogs nearby? A shooting range? Heavy truck traffic? A business that creates a lot of noise? Livestock right across the fence?

None of those automatically make a property wrong, but they're things you'll want to know before you bring horses home.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Spend a little time driving the roads surrounding a property, not just touring what's inside the fence.

Where does the manure go?

Here's one that doesn't make many real estate brochures! Horses produce a lot of manure, and once you have horses, you need a plan for it.

Can it be composted? Is there a practical place for a manure pile? Can equipment get to it? Are there neighbors close enough that placement could become an issue? It's not glamorous, but practical horse ownership rarely is.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Before buying, think about all the chores you'll do on the property, not just the fun parts.

What happens in an emergency?

If a horse is seriously injured or sick, could a veterinarian get to your property easily? If a wildfire or severe storm forced you to evacuate, could you load your horses quickly? Do you have more than one way in or out?

Those aren't fun scenarios to think about, but having horses means planning for things you hope never happen.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: A good horse property should work on the worst day, not just the best one.

Who is going to take care of this place?

Horse property doesn't take holidays. Before buying, think realistically about how much property you want to maintain. Mowing, dragging, repairs, weeds, stalls and general upkeep all take time.

Bigger isn't automatically better if maintaining it takes away from the time you actually get to spend with your horses. We have both lived the horse-property lifestyle, so we understand that part of the equation very well.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Buy the amount of property you want to maintain, not simply the most acreage you can afford.

Where is the veterinarian and the farrier?

A beautiful horse property that's way out in the country may sound perfect, but it's worth checking what services are available nearby.

How far will you travel to your veterinarian? Are good farriers serving the area? How far is the nearest equine hospital? What about feed stores and hay suppliers? Those things become pretty important once horses are actually living there.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: When you're researching an area, research the horse community around it, too.

Can you separate a horse when you need to?

This is something you may not appreciate until you need it. At some point, you may have a horse that needs to be separated because of an injury, feeding requirements, a new arrival or simply because two horses aren't getting along.

Having a small separate pen, paddock or stall can make life considerably easier.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: When touring a property, ask yourself how you would safely separate one horse from the others if you needed to.

What will winter look like here?

A horse property that looks wonderful on a beautiful sunny afternoon can be a completely different place after several days of cold rain.

Where will you feed when it's muddy? Where will horses get out of the wind? Can you reach the barn without walking through ankle-deep mud? Are exposed pipes or faucets protected from freezing? We like to imagine a property in every season, not just the season we're seeing it in.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: When you tour, picture the property during a week of the worst Texas weather, not just on showing day.

Is there room for the people, too?

When we're looking for horse property, it's easy to become so focused on what the horses need that we forget this also needs to be a good home for the family.

Where will muddy boots go? Is there a bathroom convenient to the barn? Is there somewhere to wash horse clothes and blankets without taking over the house? Where will everyone gather after spending the day outside? The best horse property works for both halves of the family: the two-legged ones and the four-legged ones.

🐴 Horse Property Tip: Your horse setup matters, but don't compromise so much on the house that you don't enjoy living there.
