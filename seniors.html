Seniors & downsizing · Parker County

Should I stay, downsize, or move closer to family?

I'm Lisa Keefe, a Broker Associate with JLA Realty and a Seniors Real Estate Specialist® (SRES®). I help seniors and their families work through the real estate side of a move at a pace that works for them. That might mean finding a one-story home, making a plan to stay where you are, or looking for a place where two generations can live together and still have some privacy.

Start with an ordinary Tuesday.

How easy is it to get in with groceries, do the laundry, and get to an appointment? What would you miss about where you live now? Write down what works, what wears you out, and what you want to keep doing. That gives you something concrete to compare when you look at another home.

What would make it easier to stay in my home?

Walk through the route you use most, from the driveway or garage to the kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, and laundry. Look for steps, narrow doorways, poor lighting, and a shower that's hard to step into. Price the changes you'd actually use, such as a handrail, better lighting, or a step-free entry. Then think about who could help with rides, yardwork, or errands if you needed it.

If you're comparing independent living, assisted living, memory care, or help at home, ask what's included, what costs extra, and what happens if your care needs change. Use the Eldercare Locator to find your local Area Agency on Aging and other services. You can also look up Medicare-listed providers through Medicare Care Compare.

Will a smaller home cost me less?

I'd put two numbers side by side. First, estimate what you'd actually keep from the sale after the mortgage payoff, selling costs, and any planned repairs. Then work out what the next place would cost each month, including taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance, and any HOA dues or community fees. Budget separately for moving, storage, and any work the new place needs before you settle in.

If you're 65 or older and moving to another Texas homestead, ask the appraisal district how to apply for the new exemption and whether a percentage of your school district tax ceiling can transfer. Get an estimate for the next home before you use the seller's current tax bill in your budget. Texas Comptroller property tax guidance

What should we talk about before moving in together?

Start with the people who'll live there. Ask what each person wants to keep doing on their own, what help they'd welcome, and what everyone can realistically commit to. Talk about privacy, guests, parking, and shared chores. Then get specific about who pays for what and what happens if someone wants to move out or needs more care. A short follow-up conversation gives everyone time to think.

A main-floor suite may give you enough separation. If you're looking at a guest house, converted garage, or room to build a second dwelling, I'd check what the property allows before counting on that space. Verify permits, deed restrictions, any HOA rules, and utility or septic capacity as applicable. If family members will share ownership or put money into the property, have a Texas attorney help you put the agreement in writing before anyone commits funds.

How do I get ready for a move?

  1. Gather the property papers. Start with your deed, mortgage information, tax and insurance records, and any survey you have. If the home is held in a trust, inherited, or someone expects to sign under a power of attorney, bring it up early so the title company and, when needed, an attorney can review who can sign.
  2. Review your family paperwork. Talk with a Texas estate planning or elder law attorney about your will or trust, financial and medical powers of attorney, and advance directives. Keep the documents secure and tell the person you've chosen where to find them.
  3. Measure before you sort. A floor plan and a tape measure can settle the question of whether the dining table is coming. Start with one room and make time for family to decide what they can take. Keep medications, important papers, and a few days of essentials with you on moving day.
  4. Plan the handoff. Decide where you'll be between closing and move-in. Line up movers, keys, utilities, and help on both ends. If you're selling and buying, build in room for dates to shift.

If you're weighing a move, I can help you estimate what your home might sell for, compare the costs of your options, and look at layouts that fit the way you live. We can start with what you'd like to make easier.
